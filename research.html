Research

My research asks how learning environments, increasingly shaped by AI, can be designed around learners to support their engagement, self-regulation, and confidence in their own capabilities. Bringing together learning theories, human-centered design, and quantitative, computational, and qualitative methods, my work spans two lines of research and a methodological strand that connects them:

  • Human-centered AI in learning and educationHow AI-mediated learning environments shape learners’ interaction, self-regulation, and confidence, and how AI support can be designed to strengthen rather than replace learners’ own thinking.
  • Learning ecologies in online learningHow online professional learning, such as micro-credentials, can sustain engagement and build self-efficacy, viewed through the learners, instructors, and institutions that shape it.
  • Learning analytics of engagement and persistenceA methodological strand across both lines: using survival analysis, clustering, and temporal analysis to understand how learners engage, persist, or disengage in online environments.

Explore examples of this work below. Feel free to reach out if you would like to learn more, especially about manuscripts under review.
