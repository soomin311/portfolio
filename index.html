Main Research Agenda

Line 1

Human-centered AI in learning and education

  • AI-mediated interaction: how AI in online discussion and collaborative learning shapes social presence, learner discourse, and shared regulation
  • AI help-seeking and self-regulation: how students’ self-efficacy and self-regulated learning relate to the way they seek help from AI and use LLM feedback in programming courses, the focus of my dissertation
  • Evidence synthesis: a systematic review of generative AI feedback and a meta-analysis of chatbots in higher education

Line 2

Learning ecologies in online learning

  • Engagement and completion: barriers and supports at three levels of the professional learning system, and early-engagement analytics that help instructors reach struggling educators in a statewide PreK–6 literacy micro-credential
  • Self-efficacy and impact at scale: how large-scale online professional learning builds teachers’ self-efficacy for teaching reading, using observational and experimental evidence
  • Institutional implementation: how institutions adopt STEM micro-credentials and support learners, in the NSF CAREER-funded ALIGN project

Teaching & Mentoring

I see teaching and mentoring as helping students grow into confident, independent learners and designers. I pair clear structure with room for students to pursue their own questions, and I make the unwritten rules of academic work visible to every student. I aim to be a consistent, approachable presence, to offer multiple ways for students to participate, and to give timely feedback that helps them take the next step.

Design & Development

Learning experience design · UX testing

Gator++: A game-based C++ learning platformSummer 2026–present

Undergraduate research project, University of Florida

A game-based learning platform for COP 3503C, an undergraduate C++ programming course, designed by undergraduate students Luis Andre M. Blanco, Jack B. Harris, and Nicholas R. Greiner, whom I co-mentor with instructor Fatemeh Tavassoli and Deniz Ercan. At each stage, students connect their design decisions back to what they heard from learners, so that learner needs shape both the problem they define and the features they build.

My role: Guiding students through the design thinking process, and planning and conducting a usability study.

  1. Learner interviews
  2. Problem definition
  3. Prototyping
  4. Usability testing

The team presented the platform at UF Innovate’s Standing InnOvation Showcase and HWCOE AI Days (October 2026).

Learning experience design · Generative AI · Prototyping

Code Your World: Generative AI meets block-based coding2025

Course team project (EME 6235 Managing Educational Projects), University of Florida, with Zifeng Liu, Seiyon Lee, and Shan Zhang

A prototype learning platform for K–12 learners that combines generative AI with block-based coding, so children can create, explore, and customize their own 2D virtual worlds. The team moved through a full design thinking cycle, from empathy and problem definition to ideation, low- and high-fidelity prototypes, and reflection.

My role: I conducted the learner needs analysis that defined the design problem and served as project manager, coordinating the timeline and task assignments across the four-person team.

  1. Dream it. Describe an idea in words, voice, or images; AI creates a world
  2. Explore it. Move through the world and interact with objects
  3. Hack it. Modify the world’s code with programming blocks
  4. Win it. Solve coding challenges to unlock new themes

View the project site, which documents each design phase and links to the Figma prototype.
